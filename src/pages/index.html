The management problem

Agents can act. Who decides how far?

Every large company hears the same promise: agents that close the books, chase invoices, buy materials and resolve tickets with little human effort.

But companies run several ERPs, a mainframe, CRM, HR and procurement suites, and hundreds of interfaces built over twenty years.

Three questions

  1. Who decides where an agent can act?
  2. Who owns a decision that spans five systems and three departments?
  3. Who explains it to the auditor and the board?

The frameworks give agents the ability to act. Leadership decides how far they’re trusted.

Three rules

The rules the book is built on

  1. The harness, not the model, sets the autonomy ceiling.

  2. Agents decide; deterministic rules post.

  3. Autonomy is earned with evidence and can be withdrawn automatically.
