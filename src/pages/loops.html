Watch agents improve a policy. You make the calls.

Real AI agents. Real computation on sample AP data. The Proposer and Supervisor are AI agents. The Guardrail harness, Evaluator and Archivist are code that runs each proposed accounts-payable exception policy over 150 synthetic sample exceptions and measures what happens. You play the AP process owner: one-way doors come to you, and you run the reframe.

The six steps

  1. 1 Expand. The Proposer agent suggests two variants of the resolution policy.
  2. 2 Guard. The guardrail harness runs each variant over the sample and checks the fitness rules. A failure stops here.
  3. 3 Decide. Two-way doors ship with an undo. One-way doors go to a named person.
  4. 4 Measure. The Evaluator computes one goal number. Its scorer is locked outside the loop.
  5. 5 Contract. The Archivist keeps the winner, archives the losers and records the lineage.
  6. 6 Reframe. People question the goal, the checks and the playbook.

What the agents run on

Sample data. 150 synthetic accounts-payable exceptions, generated once and fixed: amount, supplier size, exception type, the resolver agent’s suggestion and confidence, the correct resolution and whether a person was needed. Not real suppliers, invoices or people. Download the sample data (JSON).

The policy. Six settings: auto-approve limit, match tolerance, senior-review threshold, small-supplier protection, confidence floor and audit trace. Bank-detail changes always go to a person.

Fitness rules. Reversal rate at or under 2%, small-supplier fairness gap at or under 3 points, zero policy breaches, a complete audit trace. Any change to the scorer is blocked in code (MR-10).

Goal numbers. Cost per completed outcome: triage $0.25, automated resolution $0.60, reviewer $9.50, senior reviewer $24, reversing a wrong approval $45, other rework $18, divided by the 150 exceptions. First-pass resolution: the share resolved correctly with no person and no rework.

Limits. Each run makes at most ten AI calls. Two runs and eight instructions per visitor per hour. The site shares a daily allowance that resets at midnight UTC. Nothing you type is stored.

Real AI agents. Real computation on sample AP data. One-way doors are decided in code and explained by the Supervisor. Related controls: MR-10 TRC-05 HO-06.

One-way and two-way doors

Which changes come to a person

In the run, code sorts every change: raising the auto-approve limit above $5,000, turning off small-supplier protection or part of the audit trace, and moving invoices above the $25,000 delegated authority away from senior review are one-way doors. Two-way doors ship behind an undo. One-way doors arrive as decision cards. The line between them is the book’s ceiling: materiality times reversibility. Low materiality and easy to undo runs fast, up to L4. High materiality or hard to undo goes to a named person (HO-06).

Who said it

The ideas behind the loop

Paraphrased from public sources. Names appear as citations, not endorsements.

  • Double-loop learning

    Chris Argyris

    Single-loop learning fixes errors against a fixed goal. Double-loop learning questions the goal. His thermostat asks: “why am I set at 68 degrees?”

    Harvard Business Review, 1991. Step 6, Reframe.

  • One-way and two-way doors

    Jeff Bezos

    Some decisions are hard to undo. Most are reversible. Using the heavy process for all of them makes a company slow.

    2015 letter to shareholders (opens in a new tab). Step 3, Decide.

  • Fitness functions

    Neal Ford, Rebecca Parsons, Patrick Kua

    A fitness function is an objective integrity check on an important characteristic. It runs on every change.

    Building Evolutionary Architectures (opens in a new tab). Step 2, Guard.

  • One number, automated evaluators

    Google DeepMind, AlphaEvolve

    An evolutionary agent proposes programs. Automated evaluators verify and score each one against measurable metrics.

    Research blog, 2025 (opens in a new tab). Step 4, Measure.

  • Archive and lineage

    Sakana AI and Jeff Clune’s lab, Darwin Gödel Machine

    An archive of agent variants, where some weaker ancestors led to breakthroughs. A documented case of a faked test log was caught through the lineage of changes.

    Project page, 2025 (opens in a new tab). Step 5 and the scorer lock.

  • Permission tiers and human gates

    RAND (2026) and the Evolutionary Safety paper

    RAND recommends permission frameworks and human oversight. The paper separates proposing a change from approving it, and keeps lineage for recovery.

    arXiv 2609.31186, 2026 (opens in a new tab). Steps 3 and 5.

Questions for leaders

Who asks what

  • Board. Which doors are one-way for us, and who is the named person for each?
  • Executives. What is the one goal number? When did we last question it?
  • Managers. Which guardrails protect the work? Who may change their thresholds?
  • Staff. Which archived variants did we review? What reframe will we propose?

Lineage is a decision trace for changes: parent, change, scores and approver for every variant.
